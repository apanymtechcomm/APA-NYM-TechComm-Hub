// Planning with a Black Mirror

Tech Committee will be wrapping up the Spring Tech Table with a one-hour happy hour to celebrate the release of the Planning with a Black Mirror card game.

The American Planning Association's (APA) Technology Division created a scenario-based card game, to help planners collaboratively explore the impacts of emerging technologies and unpredictable events on communities. Please come expecting to learn and play with your fellow planners!

// Field Notes: APA NPC26 Technology Takeaways

The 2026 APA National Planning Conference in Detroit happened in late April. This event brought together over 5,000 planners to discuss key trends and challenges happening in the industry and practice. Technology related takeaways centered around:

  • AI & Machine Learning in Planning Review — Multiple sessions highlighted how AI tools are streamlining code compliance, site plan review, and zoning analysis. Some early adopters reported 30-50% faster review times for routine applications.

  • Digital Twin Technologies — Cities are building real-time digital twins to model climate resilience, traffic patterns, and utility performance. Some mobile workshops demonstrated how planners can engage communities through immersive 3D visualization.

  • Data Privacy & Ethics — A major theme across sessions: technology must serve people, not replace them. Planners emphasized the importance of maintaining human oversight in algorithmic decision-making, especially for equity-sensitive applications like housing allocation and service distribution.

  • Energy & Climate Tech Integration — Sessions on building performance analytics, distributed energy resources, and microgrid planning showed how planners can integrate energy efficiency into development review processes without adding burden.

  • Citizen Engagement Platforms — New tools are making public participation more accessible, with mobile-first interfaces and multilingual support.
